Summary

The short version

Physical AI is AI that senses the real world and acts in it through a machine. By measurable use, the most widely used physical AI today is driving, not humanoid robots: robotaxi rides and supervised self-driving count in the hundreds of thousands a week and the millions of subscribers, while humanoids are still counted in thousands of units. Inside the car, the frontier is moving from chatbots to cabin agents that perceive occupants and operate cabin hardware.

The term is new; the idea includes cars.

NVIDIA and Gartner both describe physical AI as systems that perceive, decide and act in the physical world, and both explicitly include autonomous vehicles. Gartner named it a top strategic technology trend for 2026.[1][3][4]

Driving services lead adoption, but growth is not smooth.

Tesla reports 1.48 million active FSD (Supervised) subscriptions. Waymo reached about 500,000 paid rides a week in March 2026 and has published no higher figure since. Baidu’s Apollo Go passed 23 million cumulative rides, but its driverless rides fell to roughly 0.9 million in Q2 2026 after a mass-stall incident (author’s arithmetic).[8][16][22]

Humanoids are scaling from a low base, and trackers disagree.

Global humanoid shipments were about 13,000 in 2025 by Omdia’s count. Unitree’s prospectus claims 5,511 units and first place; Omdia ranks AgiBot first with 5,168. Car factories, led by BMW–Figure, are the main proving ground.[36][35][33]

The cabin is where riders’ freed attention lands.

Cabin AI is moving from voice assistants to agents that see occupants and act on seats, climate and stops. Two designs compete: keep cabin AI fenced off from driving (Waymo) or unify them in one vehicle-level agent (Geely).[17][42]

The phone keeps identity; the cabin owns the hardware.

In-cabin physical AI can do what a phone cannot: seat-aware control of actuators, native vehicle state, and sensing that EU rules and Euro NCAP now require or reward. The phone stays the rider’s identity, payment and content hub (author’s analysis).[55][56]

Related terms, and how they fit together

TermWhat it stressesRelation to physical AI (author’s synthesis)
Traditional automationFixed, pre-programmed rules (when this sensor fires, do that)The boundary. Physical AI starts where fixed rules give way to learned behavior that adapts to what the machine senses. A robot vacuum that turns a set angle at every bump is automation; one that plans around what it sees is physical AI.
Embodied AIAI with a body that learns by interactingNear-synonym. NVIDIA uses both and includes vehicles in each; academic use stresses learning through embodiment.
World modelsModels that predict how a physical scene will evolve (e.g. NVIDIA Cosmos)The training and simulation layer: they generate synthetic data and test policies.
Vision-language-action (VLA) modelsOne model maps camera and language input to actionsThe “brain” layer, e.g. NVIDIA Alpamayo for driving.[13]
Robot foundation modelsGeneral policies across many robot bodiesA subset of VLA and policy models for robots (NVIDIA Isaac GR00T, Physical Intelligence, Skild AI).
Autonomous drivingPerception, planning and control for drivingThe most commercially mature application. End-to-end and VLA approaches are bringing it closer to robotics methods.
Spatial intelligence3D understanding and generation of spaceOverlaps with world models; investors count some world-model companies as physical AI.[27]
Working definition · author’sPhysical AI is AI that closes the loop between multimodal sensing of the real world and physical action, using learned models of space and physics, under real-world safety constraints. By this test, a cabin assistant that only chats is generative AI in a car. A cabin agent that perceives occupants (cameras, radar, microphones) and acts on cabin hardware (climate, seats, lighting, doors, a pull-over request) is physical AI.
Where definitions differ. Robot trackers (IFR, TrendForce, Omdia) count robots only. Crunchbase’s physical-AI category also includes aerospace manufacturing and energy hardware, and PitchBook’s includes defense and logistics automation. “Physical AI market” figures from different sources are therefore not comparable.

In the car: the cabin and the rider

As people move from driving to riding, the cabin is where something competes for their time and attention; cabin AI is moving from voice assistants, to assistants that see, to agentic cabins that operate hardware.

As people move from driving to riding, their time and attention are freed up, and the cabin is where something will compete for them. Cabin AI is moving through three stages. Most cars on the road are in the first; the third is where cabin AI becomes physical AI in the sense used here.

One agent or two? The architecture choice that matters most

Separated: cabin AI fenced off from driving

Example: Waymo

Waymo says Gemini works independently of the Waymo Driver, does not control the car’s movement or routing, has no access to real-time driving data, and stays inactive unless the rider engages it. The rider can ask to pull over; the driving system decides how.[17]

Unified: one vehicle-level agent

Example: Geely “1+2+N”

Geely’s framework places one vehicle-level agent over a driving agent and a cabin agent, with many task agents beneath them, so a request can span both domains.[42][40]

Author’s readSeparation limits what a cabin agent can do but keeps safety cases and liability simple. Unification enables richer requests (“find somewhere quiet to stop and recline my seat”) but ties a conversational system to safety-critical behavior. Which design wins is open, and it decides how much of the cabin a third party can ever control.

Which safety standard applies

StandardWhat it coversWhat it means for cabin agents (author’s reading)
ISO 26262 (functional safety)Hazards caused by faults in electrical and electronic systems: hardware failures and software errors.[77]A cabin agent fenced off from driving, as at Waymo, stays outside the driving safety case. A unified agent that can influence driving brings a conversational model into SOTIF analysis, where its ways of misunderstanding a request become a safety question.
ISO 21448 (SOTIF)Hazards when the system works as designed but its performance falls short, for example misreading a situation, including reasonably foreseeable misuse.[76]

Risks and open questions. Privacy of cabin cameras and microphones under closed-loop rules; liability if a cabin agent influences how the car drives; the cost of extra computing (add-on “AI boxes” versus single chips for cabin and driving); riders who prefer quiet (Waymo offers an opt-in assistant and a calm mode); and fleet-wide software reliability, shown when the Wuhan stall left riders waiting up to about two hours.[60]

Cabin agents with physical control

Gemini in Waymo leaving beta; BMW’s Alexa+ assistant in customer cars; GM’s native assistant; and whether any operator lets a cabin agent control seats or windows, or links cabin and driving agents.

Method, limits and flags

Read before citing

Question. What is physical AI, who are the main players across the stack, which products are used most, and how does it apply to the car, with the cabin and rider experience as the main lens?

Scope. Global, whole stack. Placed under Shifts / Mobility on this site. Neutral and industry-wide: no recommendation is made to any company.

Sources. Official and primary sources first (company filings and releases, regulators, standards bodies), then analyst research, then trade press. Each reference carries its type and date. Company-reported, analyst and forecast numbers are labeled. Two figures were re-checked against primary documents for this page: Baidu’s H1 2026 rides (2026 Interim Report) and Euro NCAP’s occupant-monitoring points (protocol v1.2).

What this report is not. The ranking, the working definition, the stack diagram, maturity ratings and the “why not just the phone” and opportunity tables are the author’s analysis, not sourced figures.

  2. TrendForce China 2026 outlook appears as +94% output (April), +107% shipments (Q3 report) and more than 100,000 units (August).
  3. IFR 2025 installations are given as “more than 600,000” and 603,307; China’s share as 54% and 59% in different summaries.
  4. Waymo’s Gemini. Early leaked prompts (Dec 2025) and a 2026 trade article say it cannot control windows or seats; Waymo’s July 2026 post says “cabin controls” with an air-conditioning example. Seat control is unverified.
  5. Waymo fleet size varies by source: about 3,000 (Mar 2026), 3,871 (a June filing, secondary) and about 4,000 (Sep 2026, secondary). Not used in the body.
  6. Secondary only: the China robotaxi permit pause and its reported resumption; WeRide’s fleet; Li Auto’s usage figures; Euro NCAP’s wider 100-point Safe Driving category; Physical Intelligence funding.
  7. Company claims not independently verified: Waymo safety statistics, Baidu safety rates, Cerence’s installed base, AgiBot production counts (production is not deployment), NVIDIA benchmark claims for Cosmos 3, Zeekr order counts.
  8. Forecasts, not facts: Gartner’s 2035 prediction, IFR’s 2029 outlook, TrendForce and bank humanoid forecasts, and Waymo’s 1 million-a-week target.
