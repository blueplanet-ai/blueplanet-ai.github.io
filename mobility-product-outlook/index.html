Summary

The short version

Customers now judge cars by the standard their phones set. They expect a product that improves after purchase, one assistant that follows them everywhere, a car that works with the rest of their devices, honest pricing for software, and controls that stay simple. Over the next decade this moves the definition of a car away from a finished machine sold by model year, toward a long-lived hardware platform that is defined, sold and improved through software.

Phones reset the clock: years of updates are now normal.

Google and Samsung promise seven years of software updates for their flagship phones. In the US, 52% of car buyers say regular over-the-air updates would make them keep a car longer; in China it is 72% (Deloitte 2026).

Consumer electronics companies already build cars, and fast.

Xiaomi delivered more than 600,000 cars in its first 22 months (trade press reporting Xiaomi). Huawei’s alliance brands delivered 589,107 cars in 2025 and shipped 141 over-the-air updates in that year. AlixPartners finds China’s new operating model brings cars to market twice as fast.

More technology is not the same as a better product.

J.D. Power’s 2026 study finds passenger screens among the most problematic technologies, and 35% of owners who have one have never tried it. BMW dropped paid heated-seat activation in 2023 after customers felt they had “paid double”. The winners make life simpler and charge for services that are actually new.

    Robotaxi and shared-vehicle interiors

    When the product is the ride

    In a robotaxi nobody owns the car, so the product is the trip: getting in, feeling safe, staying in control, and arriving. Purpose-built vehicles are now in service, and their cabins show a different set of priorities from private cars.

    VehicleStatusCabin and controlsDesign priority it shows

    Design principles for shared cabins

    Author’s synthesis of the vehicles above

    • Visible control. With no driver, each seat needs its own way to start the ride, ask for help or stop: Zoox puts Help and Pull Over on a touchscreen at every seat, plus an emergency call button above each row.
    • Easy in, easy out. Flat floors, low step-in, wide doors and grab bars (Waymo Ojai) matter more than horsepower.
    • Built to be cleaned and abused. Waymo says Ojai is designed to handle “the use and abuse of hundreds of thousands of riders”.
    • The phone is the key and the profile. Booking, music and settings arrive from the rider’s own account, so the cabin can stay simple.
    • Safety rethought for new seat layouts. Zoox’s rider manual lists ceiling, frontal, rear, seat-side and seat-pan airbags for rear-facing and forward-facing seats.

    Why this order

    Author’s judgment

    • Ranks 1–6 already show mass-market adoption data and directly change routines: how people get information, travel, manage their weight and pay for energy.
    • Software-defined vehicles rank only 7th for daily life, but they are what lets ranks 1, 3 and 6 work inside a car, and they underpin most of the framework above.
    • Humanoid robots rank lower than the hype suggests because the IFR counted only about 7,000 sold in 2025.
    • Ranks 12–15 were not researched in depth in this edition, so their scores rest on weaker evidence.

    Wildcards and forecasting humility

    What could change the picture

    Wildcards

    No credible published probabilities were found, so none are given

    • Big Tech takes the cabin. If phone-projection systems such as CarPlay Ultra cover every screen in most new cars, the carmaker’s own interface becomes a thin layer.
    • The opposite: carmakers close the door. GM plans to remove CarPlay and Android Auto across its line-up; if buyers accept that, native systems win.
    • AI progress stalls, or jumps. METR’s task benchmark is close to its measurable limit, so 2027–2030 is uncertain in both directions.
    • An electricity-price backlash. If power bills keep rising, home charging and car-to-grid could become either a selling point or a political target.
    • A privacy shock. A major in-car data breach or camera misuse case could slow AI cabins and data-based services.
    • A faster US–China tech split that forces separate products for each bloc, and a major robotaxi accident that resets permits.

    Caveats

    Read before relying on the outlook

    • Five-to-ten-year technology forecasts have often been wrong in both directions. Robotaxi timelines were over-promised in 2015–2020, while electric car and generative AI adoption beat many forecasts.
    • Survey answers are not purchases. Stated interest in software and services has often exceeded what buyers later pay for.
    • Several case-study figures come from trade press reporting company statements rather than from the companies’ own filings; they are labelled in the sources.
